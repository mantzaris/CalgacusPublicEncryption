 the Dewey Decimal System

If you've ever spent time in a library, you may have noticed that books are shelved in a seemingly organized manner. There's a system behind this organization, and it's called the Dewey Decimal System.

Invented by Melvil Dewey in 1876, the Dewey Decimal System is a method of organizing books by assigning each one a unique number based on its subject. The system is based on the idea of grouping books by topic, with each topic having its own specific range of numbers.

Here's a breakdown of how it works:

1. Books are grouped into ten broad categories, known as the "major classes." These major classes are further broken down into smaller categories, known as the "classes."
2. Each class is then broken down into more specific topics, known as "divisions."
3. Within each division, there are even more specific topics, known as "sections."
