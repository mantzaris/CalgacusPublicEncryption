 the people and the tasks involved
In an apple orchard, there are various tasks involved in sorting the apples. These tasks are carried out by a team of people who work together to ensure that the apples are sorted correctly and of the best quality.
The sorting process usually begins with a visual inspection of the apples. This is done by the "pickers" who are responsible for plucking the apples from the trees. They carefully select the apples that are ripe and of the right quality, and place them in a bucket or onto a tray.
Once the apples have been harvested, they are transferred to a sorting area where they are inspected for any imperfections. This is done by the "sorters" who work together to ensure that the apples are free from any defects such as bruises, blemishes, and pests. They use a combination of visual inspection and manual handling to check the apples for any imperfections.
The apples that are deemed to be of the best quality are then transferred to a packing area where they are packed into boxes or bins. This is done by the "packers" who are responsible for preparing the apples for distribution to the market. They carefully arrange the apples in the containers, making sure that they are secure and not damaged during transportation.
In addition to the people involved in the sorting and packing process, there are also other tasks that need to be carried out at the orchard. These include pruning the trees, fertilizing the soil, and spraying the apples with pesticides to protect them from pests and diseases. These tasks are carried out by the "orchardists" who are responsible for the overall maintenance of the orchard.
