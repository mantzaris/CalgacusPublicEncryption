Wind effects on waves around a stone pier
As the wind blows over the water, it transfers energy to the waves, causing them to change direction and shape. When waves encounter a stone pier, the wind can further modify the wave patterns, creating various effects.
1. Pier shadow zone: As the wind blows parallel to the pier, it creates a shadow zone on the windward side, where waves are slowed or even stopped due to the pier's obstruction. This zone can be especially pronounced in areas with strong winds and steep waves.
2. Pier-steeper waves: Wind blowing perpendicular to the pier can create pier-steeper waves, which are more turbulent and higher than the surrounding waves. This phenomenon occurs when the wind blows directly onto the pier, creating a localized turbulent spot.
3. Wave refraction and diffraction: Wind can also cause waves to bend around the pier, a process known as refraction. Additionally, wind can scatter waves as they pass around the pier, creating a diffraction pattern. This refracted and diffracted wave energy can be more intense on the leeward side, making it a potential area for wave energy harvesting.
