 "A brass compass is a navigational tool that uses the Earth's magnetic field to determine direction. A small, magnetized needle is suspended inside the compass case, where it aligns itself with the Earth's magnetic field. The needle is designed to move freely in two dimensions, allowing it to rotate to point towards the magnetic north pole. The compass is calibrated with a degree scale on the outside of the case, allowing the user to determine the direction of north, south, east, and west. The compass can be used to navigate by following the direction indicated by the needle, or to determine the bearing between two locations." Museum Note, Smithsonian Museum of American History 2006. This note is not only educational but also provides valuable information for researchers, historians, and others who are interested in learning more about the history and function of brass compasses. The note also demonstrates that brass compasses are still an important tool for navigation and continue to be used today.
