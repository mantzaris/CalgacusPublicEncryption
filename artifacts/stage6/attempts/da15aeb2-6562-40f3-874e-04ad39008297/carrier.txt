 "A brass compass is a navigational instrument that uses the magnetic properties of the earth to determine its direction. It consists of a magnetic needle that is designed to align itself with the earth's magnetic field. The needle is free to move in any direction, but it is attracted to the magnetic north pole. When you place the compass on a flat surface, the needle will settle in alignment with the magnetic field, pointing towards the north pole. By reading the direction of the needle, you can determine your direction of travel.
The compass also has a dial marked with cardinal directions (North, South, East, and West) and sometimes other intermediate directions, such as the four cardinal directions (N, NE, E, SE, etc.). When the magnetic needle points to North, the compass dial indicates the direction as a direct line from the center of the compass. The direction of the magnetic needle can be read from the dial.
The brass compass is an ancient navigational tool, dating back to the Han Dynasty in China around 206 BCE. It has been used by explorers and travelers for centuries to find their way and determine their direction. Despite the advent of modern GPS technology, the brass compass remains a useful and iconic navigational tool." This note is from the collection of a historical museum, and is meant to educate visitors about the functioning of a brass compass.
