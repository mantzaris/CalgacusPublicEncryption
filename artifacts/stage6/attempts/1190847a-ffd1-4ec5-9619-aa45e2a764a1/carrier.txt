 the local scale, my own locality
On the West coast of Scotland, in particular around this stretch of the Firth of Clyde, wave parameters such as wave height, period, and direction play a crucial role in influencing both the marine environment and human activities. In this blog, I will focus on the local scale, specifically a stone pier in my own locality.
The pier, situated in the mouth of a small bay, is an important structure for the local fishing industry. Anglers and fishermen often use the pier as a launching point for their vessels, and it provides a convenient spot for observing the marine environment. A thorough understanding of wave dynamics around the pier is essential for monitoring the safety and efficiency of these activities.
Wave height is a critical factor to consider when it comes to the safety of the pier and the vessels that use it. Large waves can cause damage to the pier and even make it difficult for boats to navigate safely. On the other hand, small waves may not pose a significant threat to the structure or the vessels. In this context, the wave height is an essential parameter for monitoring and managing the pier.

Wave direction is another crucial factor to consider when it comes to the pier. The direction of the waves can influence the stability of the pier and the safety of the vessels that use it. A consistent wave direction can ensure a stable structure, while an inconsistent wave direction can cause the pier to be subjected to more stress and potentially lead to damage.

Wave period is also an important consideration for the pier. The longer the wave period, the more energy the wave carries, and this can affect the structure of the pier. A longer wave period can cause more stress on the pier and potentially lead to damage.
