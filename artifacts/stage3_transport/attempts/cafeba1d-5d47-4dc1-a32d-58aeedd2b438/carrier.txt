 Dewey Decimal System (DDC) and Library of Congress Classification (LCC)
by Book & Price | Jul 19, 2022 | Library Systems | 0 comments
When you enter a library, you might be surprised by the sheer number of books it contains. With millions of books, it can be overwhelming to find the one you need. That’s where a library’s cataloging and classification system come into play. In this article, we’ll discuss two popular methods used by libraries to organize their collections: the Dewey Decimal System (DDC) and the Library of Congress Classification (LCC).

### Dewey Decimal System (DDC)

The Dewey Decimal System is a widely used classification system developed by Melvil Dewey in the 19th century. It’s a numerical classification system that organizes books on a shelf by their subject or topic. The system is based on a hierarchical structure, with ten main classes (000-199) related to general works, mathematics, science, technology, social sciences, history, philosophy, literature, language, and the arts.

Each class is further divided into more specific subclasses, using decimal points to separate main classes, subclasses, and topics. This creates a system that allows books to be sorted and shelved accordingly. For example:

* 000 – General works (e.g., essay collections, bibliographies)
* 500 – Science (e.g., biology, physics, mathematics)
* 700 – Arts and recreation (e.g., literature, music, theatre)
* 900 – History and geography (e.g., world history, local history, geography)

The Dewey Decimal System is widely used in public and school libraries, as it provides a simple and accessible way to navigate through a collection. However, its limitations include the need for frequent updates and the potential for confusion when multiple editions of a work exist.
