 Libraries use the Dewey Decimal Classification system (DDC) to organize their books. The system is based on the topic of the book, not the author. The numbers are divided into classes and subclasses, which help the books to be shelved in a organized manner. Books with similar topics are grouped together in the same section. This makes it easier for readers to find books on a particular subject. For example, books on computers would be shelved together in the 005 section.
A librarian's work is a constant process of organization and maintenance. They continually process new materials, shift and adjust collections as they grow and evolve, and must stay up-to-date with the constantly changing world of books and information. In order to proficiently perform their duties, librarians must have a solid understanding of the Dewey Decimal Classification system and how it is used to organize libraries.
