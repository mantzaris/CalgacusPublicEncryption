# Stage 8 claim boundaries

Stage 8 extends one public carrier ceiling under a new registered profile. It introduces no new arithmetic-coding or cryptographic primitive. The fixed Stage 7 arithmetic implementation and the shared rank16 canonical-prefix candidate-selection code remain unchanged. Attribution, source revision and licensing limits remain in [stage7_comparator_audit.md](stage7_comparator_audit.md); this stage does not copy unlicensed upstream code or reopen internal manuscript reconciliation.

| Claim | Status and exact support | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| The three Stage 7 ceiling aborts are consistent with insufficient information accumulation. | Independent reconstruction of every interval, stable bit and pending bit from all four historical traces; [diagnosis](stage8_capacity_diagnosis.md). | Evidence about these trajectories, not every possible arithmetic implementation or model. |
| Large pending underflow debt or finite-precision rounding explains those deficits. | Not supported: terminal pending debt is 0/0/2 and cumulative interval rounding changes information by less than 0.000025 bits. | Underflow release still affects local timing; it is not absent. |
| Low-information stretches can delay packet completion substantially. | The historical 32-byte HPKE case contains a 256-token block with no stable-bit release and 0.115071 selected-information bits. | Context and packet both differ across qualifications; no HPKE-structure causal claim. |
| A larger ceiling preserves the earlier arithmetic/model trajectory. | All four Stage 8 diagnostic prefixes match old token IDs, candidate-order hashes, frequency tables and arithmetic states exactly. | Same frozen hardware/backend/model/context/packet; not cross-platform determinism. |
| One formerly failed HPKE packet can complete at the new ceiling. | Exact original 100-byte envelope at 1942 tokens, followed by fresh-process recovery and original-binding authentication. | A diagnostic re-embedding, not a fresh Stage 8-bound message or repair of the old 1536-token outcome. |
| The new ceiling makes every historical packet fit. | False in this allocation: the two 196-byte packets still abort at 1984 with 1426 and 1529 stable bits of 1568. | No second ceiling or alternative-context search. |
| The extended profile supports every fresh packet. | False: F recovered 4/4 at each size; R recovered 2/4 at 32 bytes and 0/4 at 128 bytes. Six fresh arithmetic capacity aborts remain. | Four development-informed contexts and one repetition; no population reliability guarantee. |
| Extending the budget helps within a fresh trajectory. | R32 completion is 0/4, 1/4, 1/4, 2/4 at 512/1024/1536/1984, respectively; its two completed carriers stop at 563 and 1783 tokens. | Nested observations, not independent experiments or new ciphertexts compared with old ones. |
| Actual-text recovery works independently of encoder state. | Five predetermined main receiver processes recover exactly: four F and one R. | Fixed-orientation surprisal AUC is lower for R in this sample. | Complete canonical-token surprisal AUC is 1.0 for F (eight pairs) and 0.5 for R (two pairs); joint-support F/R values are also 1.0/0.5. | R interval [0,1], opposite single-pair orderings across its two contexts, delivery and length conditioning; not evidence of concealment or a stable cross-method effect. |
| A public completion/stopping predicate recognizes delivered R messages here. | Both R messages pass; all eight R-associated B controls fail (six incomplete packets, two trailing-token rejections). Matched-pair format AUC 1.0. | Two matched pairs only; midpoint-suffix and KEM-specific effects are not isolated, and tags are not authenticated. |
| Public extraction and canonical KEM representation always distinguish carriers. | Refuted as a universal inference: all eight F controls pass both predicates, so their AUCs are 0.5 here. | Applies to the specified probability-weighted admissible controls, not arbitrary cover distributions. |
| The observed rate/cost tradeoff is operationally material. | R32 successes require 563/1783 tokens versus F's 200. Its mean attempt useful rate is 0.149571 bits/token versus F's 1.28; R128 useful recovery is zero. | Successful-message, attempt-level and pooled delivered rates differ; costs include startup and failed jobs. |
| Public extraction authenticates HPKE tags or reveals encrypted plaintext. | Not claimed. Format/termination and canonical encapsulation representation are public predicates. | HPKE Base mode supplies no sender authentication; no key or authentication outcome enters the observer. |
| Probability matching guarantees concealment or a low AUC proves it. | Not claimed. Complete-message scores use explicitly length-conditioned, probability-weighted admissible controls. | The restricted distribution is not ordinary human or unrestricted model traffic; abort conditioning and limited contexts matter. |
| This is a new, publication-ready steganographic construction. | Not claimed. The candidate contribution is a controlled empirical account of finite-packet capacity, actual-text recovery and public recognition. | Novelty and regular-paper sufficiency require review of the measured result and its relation to established principles. |

The main analysis keeps complete received messages primary. No fixed64-token encapsulation exclusion is applied to arithmetic output. All body-region scores are unavailable. Fixed higher-is-carrier AUC orientations are not changed after results are observed. Small or degenerate cluster intervals are descriptive, not guarantees.

The final [report](../STAGE8_CAPACITY_COMPARISON_REPORT.md), [summary](../artifacts/stage8/summary.json), [tables](../artifacts/stage8/tables.md) and [manifest](../manifests/stage8_evidence.json) contain exact denominators, conditional intervals, source revisions and records. Stage 8 used 43 attempts, 5156.875482143 conservative GPU-job seconds and 75028 evaluated tokens; no additional work is launched.

**Strongest defensible paper claim:** under this frozen public model and canonical admissible support, correctly implemented finite-packet arithmetic transport exhibits severe trajectory-dependent capacity limits; one safe ceiling extension enables some 32-byte recovery while leaving 128-byte failures, and the two delivered messages have lower aggregate fixed-orientation surprisal AUC (with interval [0,1]) yet retain recognizable completion/stopping structure against the specified controls. This is measured supporting evidence, not a new algorithm or a cleared novelty claim.

**Missing result for a broad regular-paper conclusion:** a sufficiently supported delivered-message comparison across contexts and payload classes that can separate a transferable capacity/recognition effect from this two-message survivor sample. This package does not automatically commission more GPU work or another ceiling. The smallest next action is external review of the existing finite-packet diagnosis, scoring contract and claim scope.
